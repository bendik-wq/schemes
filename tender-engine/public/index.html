There are two kinds of supplier in a tender round.

The first reads the ITT on Friday afternoon, hands it to the two engineers who know the product, and loses them for a week. Someone copies last year's answers out of an old Word file. The deviation list gets written the night before closing. Half the time they find the clause that kills the bid on page 63, after the work is done.

The second knows within an hour of receiving the inquiry what is being asked, which approvals they are missing, and whether to bid at all. Their answers come from one approved library, with certificates that are in date. Their engineers spend their time on the technical solution, which is the part the buyer actually scores.

Both have the same product, the same people and the same JQS profile. The difference is the system around the bid.

If bids eat your best people, the problem isn't your people.

It isn't that your engineers write slowly, or that your sales manager doesn't try hard enough, or that you need another bid coordinator at NOK 900,000 a year.

Offshore buyers send documents written for contractors with bid departments, and small and mid-sized suppliers answer them by hand. The software that exists was built for something else. Tender search tools only see public notices, and most operator RFQs are invite-only. Generic proposal software was built for US software security questionnaires. None of the tools we looked at builds a NORSOK compliance matrix or a deviation list, or keeps your Z-018 and Transparency Act answers in one place.

So the work lands on the few people who understand both the product and the paperwork. That's what Northbid takes off them.

Answer

Your answers, checked twice.

Each requirement is matched to an entry in your library: certificates, HSE statistics, documentation practice, standard terms. Set each row to comply, partial, deviation or clarify, then export the matrix and a cover letter draft. Missing facts stay as [placeholders]; it never makes up references. Every RFQ is read twice by two different methods, and anything only the second pass found is flagged for you to check.

Questions

Most of our RFQs come through Ariba and Ivalua, not public portals. Does it help?

Yes, that's the main use. You download the inquiry documents from the operator's portal and drop them into Northbid. Tender watch only covers the public notices on top of that. Northbid doesn't log in to operator portals on your behalf.

Is our data safe? Bids are confidential.

Each client gets their own workspace and database on Cloudflare, behind a password. When the Claude reader is on, documents are sent to Anthropic's API for processing; Anthropic doesn't train on API data by default. If a document can't leave your walls, the built-in rule reader works without sending text anywhere. We sign an NDA and a data processing agreement before day 1.

Does it write our bids?

It writes first drafts: the matrix, the questions for the buyer and a cover letter, using only your profile and library. Anything it doesn't know stays as a [placeholder]. Your people check every row and own the submission.

Which standards does it recognise?

NORSOK (for example M-501, S-006, Z-018), ISO, IEC, EN, API, DNV, ASME, ATEX/IECEx and IMCA references are picked out and compared with the approvals on your profile.

We already pay for Mercell or Doffin alerts.

Keep them if you like them. Search tools find public notices; Northbid is mainly for what happens after the inquiry arrives, which is where the hours go.

Norwegian-language tenders?

Yes. The reader handles Norwegian and English documents, and the cover letter draft follows the language of the RFQ.
