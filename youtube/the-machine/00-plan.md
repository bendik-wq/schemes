Built on the [Daniel Iles A–Z playbook](../../research/daniel-iles/playbook.md) (90 of his videos, every rule linked to the timestamp). Every episode teaches one thing he teaches, generalises it so any high-ticket service founder can use it, and then works as a VSL for one service.

## 2. The red thread

**The Machine.** Daniel Iles took Viral Coach to about $5M/month with one simple machine: *offer → ads → page → application → show-up → call → numbers*. Most founders selling high-ticket services have two or three of the seven parts. The parts they're missing are why growth stalls at referrals.

- Every episode opens with the machine diagram, one part lit up: "This is part 3 of 7."
- Every episode teaches that part well enough that a viewer could build it alone.
- Every episode ends with the same turn: "You can build this yourself. Everything you need is in this video. Or we build all seven parts for you in 30 days."
- Every episode hands off to the next part ("a page is useless if the wrong people fill in the form, and that's the next video").
- Episode 1 is the map. Episode 20 is the honest case for buying the machine instead of building it.

### The circular proof

The application under each video is itself built with the machine: scored form, routing, reminder stack, dashboard. Say so on camera (Daniel uses this on purpose: "this works because it got you here"). It's true, and it's the strongest proof available before there are client case studies.

### The source, credited

Each episode names Daniel Iles as the source of the specific lesson and links the exact timestamp in the description. His numbers are *his*, never presented as ours. The pitch is ours: "I studied 90 of his videos. Here's the part that matters, how to use it if you don't do $5M/month, and how we build it for you."

## 3. Rules for every script

1. **Honesty.** No made-up clients, results or income claims. Daniel's figures are attributed to him ("he says", "in his business"). Where our own proof will go, the script says `[PROOF: …]` with what to film once it exists. Until then, use the founding-client line: *"We're taking a small number of founding clients at this price in exchange for a filmed case study."*
2. **No affiliation implied.** We are not Daniel Iles, Viral Coach or partners of theirs. One line in each description says so.
3. **Daniel's VSL timing.** What we do, why it's different, proof and the CTA all land inside the first ~3 minutes (a short soft pitch at ~2:30). Then the teaching. Then the full pitch. Then a short "for skeptics" block (budget, authority, need, timing).
4. **Give the recipe early.** No "recipe blog" openings. The lesson starts by 0:45.
5. **Say who it's not for, more than once.**
6. **One CTA.** The application. Free checklist only for non-fits.
7. **Voice.** First person, calm, plain English, short sentences, no hype words. Daniel's own tone: dry, numbers-first, a bit contrarian. 8–14 minutes spoken (≈1,300–2,000 words).
8. **Ad compliance.** No personal-attribute call-outs ("Are you struggling…?"), no income claims in the first line, because the best hooks will be cut into ads.

## 6. Running it like Daniel would

- **Measure applications and booked calls per video, not views** (Daniel: "Why I don't get views"; he judges content by the bank balance).
- Every video's best 3 hooks become ads (founder face → this VSL-style video is fine as the landing video, because the person in the ad is the person in the video, C2.4).
- Log why calls are lost. Every quarter, re-cut the top-loss-reason episode and pin it.
- Swap `[PROOF: …]` placeholders as founding-client case studies come in. Re-upload the CTA segment only, not the whole video.
