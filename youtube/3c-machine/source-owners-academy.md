## Teaching points from the masterclass

- **Two wrong ways / the third way:** saving for a deposit (years, then the bank says no or PE outbids you); raising from investors (months per cheque, give away equity, control and the exit). The third way is the 3C model.
- **Burden of ownership:** a generation of owners in traditional industries is ready to retire. They don't want to wait 16 weeks for a bank; they want out and to be looked after. Vendor finance can pay above market (6–7× vs the usual 3–5×) and close in 2–4 weeks instead of 6–16. Never say "vendor finance" to a seller; say "getting paid over time".
- **The speed run:** business making $250K profit ÷ 1.5 DSCR = ~$166K/yr to the seller; over 10 years = $1.66M, well above most offers. (1:33:06–1:36:53)
- **Steal credibility:** no track record → borrow it. Board with founders' equity (2–10% each), found via LinkedIn and cold contact. Josh did it at 25 with no network.
- **Debt over dilution:** debt is more available than investors (a bank is set up to lend); only safe inside the 1.5× DSCR rule.
- **Closing process:** cold outreach (calls best); before an offer get 3 years of financials, matching tax returns, revenue breakdown by FY; offer 3–5× profit in an LOI; proper DD; top-tier lawyers.
- **Negotiation:** best tactic is to walk away; second best is a time limit ("24 hours to accept").
- **Roll-up / multiple arbitrage:** buy ten businesses at ~4× profit, grow and combine, sell the group at 8, 10, 15×. Business vs real estate: cash flow, time, multiple arbitrage. (0:15:09–0:18:55)
- **Decide at 60%:** "There is never a perfect moment." Passive vs active risk. (2:10:14)
- **Minority holders:** drag-along, no veto or voting control for minorities, set up from the start. (2:01:07)
- **Personal guarantees:** larger deals (over ~$5M) often don't need one; asset-rich businesses give the bank its security — "typically; every lender and deal is different". (1:50:50)
- **Board first or deal first:** either works; Josh recommends board first (executive summary PDF + handshake equity). (2:37:24)
- **Free content vs joining:** Josh agrees the model is learnable for free; the case is speed and community: "there's so much value to just being able to ask someone". Then: "You've been watching for months. How many offers have you sent?" (2:26:43, 2:19:06)
- **Time, money, energy:** "I've had people pay me $20–30K for advisory and still not take action… where your energy goes decides whether this works."
- **US/UK:** lender network in the US; Josh can be a director only in Australia, elsewhere advisor or equity holder.
