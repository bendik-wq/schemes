# Daniel Iles style guide: how he hooks, structures and explains

Use this guide to write Josh Li episodes in Daniel Iles' format. It covers how Daniel delivers a video, not what he teaches. His teachings are in `research/daniel-iles/playbook.md`. Persuasion structure comes from the Sultanic skills. This guide covers the delivery that wraps both.

Evidence comes from all 92 titles in `video-index.md`, about 45 videos read in `video-notes.md` (short talks, mid-length breakdowns and trainings up to 2h39), and playbook sections B2 and C2. The notes paraphrase him, so treat opening "lines" below as reconstructed beats, not verbatim quotes.

**In one sentence:** Daniel is an operator showing his own numbers. He opens on a result and the mechanism behind it, explains each idea with one analogy and one piece of math, admits where it doesn't apply, and pitches quietly at the end to a narrow group.

---

## 1. Title formulas

Of his 92 titles, 55 contain a dollar figure, about half are first person ("I", "my", "we"), and 22 end in a parenthetical tag. The number is usually his current monthly run rate, so it rises over time: titles say $3M during the $3M period and $5M later.

| # | Formula | Real examples | Fill-in template |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Run rate + owned asset.** "My $X/mo [system]", "How I [verb] [thing] at $X" | "My $5 million per month sales system"; "My $3 Million per month sales script"; "How I manage a $3 million per month sales team" | "My [proof number] [asset]" |
| 2 | **Paid X → here's the payoff** | "I paid 20k to learn this in 5 minutes"; "I spent $1M on Facebook ads - here is what actually works"; "We Paid Iman Gadzhi $18k Each. Here's What We Learned." | "I [paid/spent] [cost] [doing X]. Here's what [worked / I got]" |
| 3 | **Level vs level** | "$10k/mo vs $1m/mo paid ads"; "$100K/m VS $1M/m offers"; "Selling DFY vs DIY (I make $3.5M/mo from this)"; "Organic content vs paid ads" | "[Small level] vs [big level] [topic]" |
| 4 | **The complete map** | "Every revenue level from $10k to $5M / month explained (51 minute breakdown)"; "Every number I tracked to hit $3m/mo"; "These 64 tools are the secret to my $3M per month business" | "Every [level/number/step] from [A] to [B] explained" |
| 5 | **N mistakes / lessons, with a cost** | "Top 5 mistakes when hitting $1M / month"; "5 mistakes costing you $30k/mo"; "I lost millions learning these harsh business lessons"; "8 questions improved my business by 2,629%" | "[N] [mistakes/questions] that [cost / made] [number]" |
| 6 | **Contrarian verdict** | "VSL's are dead"; "agencies suck"; "Hustle culture is broken (do this instead)"; "Being "smart" is killing your business"; "Most guarantees suck, here's how I make millions from them" | "[Accepted thing] is [dead/broken/killing you]" |
| 7 | **Danger / gate** | "Why $10k per month is a dangerous place to be"; "You don't have a real business unless you can buy customers"; "You won't make 1M/mo until you learn this" | "Why [comfortable state] is dangerous" / "You won't [goal] until [X]" |
| 8 | **Copy this asset** | "This landing page makes me $116,000 per day (copy it)"; "These emails made me $720K (just copy them)"; "Here's How We're Scaling to $10M/Month (Just Copy Us)" | "This [asset] [did result] (copy it)" |
| 9 | **Time-box contract** | "These 6 minutes will make you more successful"; "Give me 4 minutes and you will never need motivation again"; "Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 40 Minutes" | "Give me [N] minutes and [outcome]" |
| 10 | **Milestone or confession** | "I just made 5 million dollars in one month."; "I failed..."; "Why I disappeared..."; "How I went broke trying to get rich... and then got rich."; "The disappointment of making $5 million per month" | "I [milestone/failure]..." with a trailing ellipsis |
| 11 | **Paradox** | "How being "boring" makes me $1.5M profit every month"; "How I close $3 million with a 1.8% close rate"; "Why I don't get views" | "How [unattractive trait/number] produces [result]" |
| 12 | **Result + timeframe, step by step** | "$0 to $500K in 90 days (checklist scaling)"; "How I scaled my ads from $0 to $1M/mo (step-by-step)" | "[$0] to [result] in [time] ([method tag])" |

**Parenthetical tags he uses to set expectations:** (copy it), (step-by-step), (51 minute breakdown), (internal company vid), (uploaded for free), (Raw Conversation), (if I had to start over), (the truth), (do this instead).

**Adapting for Josh.** The writer brief bans income claims in the first line and "are you…" call-outs. Swap Daniel's revenue number for a *deal* or *structure* number, put the disclaimer on screen, and lean on formulas 3, 4, 6, 7, 9 and 11, which don't need an income claim. Examples:
- "Every way to fund a business purchase, from 0% to 100% down, explained"
- "Bank loan vs seller finance"
- "Why buying a business with cash is a dangerous move"
- "Give me 8 minutes and you'll never pay full price for a business"
- "This one-page summary got [JOSH: result] (copy it)"

## 2. Opening hooks (first 15–30 seconds)

The first nugget in almost every video sits at 0:00–0:30. **He states the point or the proof before he says anything else.** He doesn't greet, recap the channel or ask viewers to like and subscribe. His VSL rules say the same: hook, promise, proof, then the point, and "give the recipe early" (the "recipe blog" problem, [$10M VSL 1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_3-WQstsN5I&t=92s)).

Six opening moves, often stacked two at a time:

1. **Thesis plus the number that proves it.** In *I paid 20k to learn this in 5 minutes* (0:00), he says monthly fees are the wrong model, and that changing them took the business from $1.3M to $3M a month within 30 days. *$100K/m VS $1M/m offers* (0:00) opens on every revenue step and the offer change behind each one.
2. **Data-source credential.** *Why $10k per month is a dangerous place to be* (0:00) opens on roughly 2,000 business-owner conversations a month. *How to close $1M/mo as a sales rep* (0:00) opens on one rep closing $1M from about 200 calls, out of 10,000+ calls in total. The credential is how much he has seen, not how good he is.
3. **Blunt maxim in his own words.** *The truth about running a business with your bros* (0:00): partnerships fail because "the conditions change over time and the terms don't." *A message to my competitors...* (0:00): "All the stuff that you can't copy… is what's actually important." *Why your landing page doesn't convert* (0:00): the first few words on the page are "the most important words in your business."
4. **Low point.** *Every number I tracked to hit $3m/mo* (0:00) opens on $500K in debt, $400 in the bank and half the sales team laid off. *Got fit - started making $3M per month* (0:00) opens 50 lb heavier with $500K of debt. *Give me 4 minutes...* (0:00) opens on $400 in the bank and then $210–280K revenue days.
5. **Everyday image.** *Why do you turn the music down when looking for your turn?* (0:00) opens on turning the music down to read road signs.
6. **Stakes or cost of the mistake.** *$10k/mo vs $1m/mo paid ads* (0:00–0:31) opens on one mistake that wasted 40% of a $500K/month client's spend. *I just made 5 million dollars in one month.* (0:30) says he knew the fix in February and delaying it cost about $6M.

By 0:30 he has usually moved to the first teaching point. Examples: the book analogy in *I paid 20k* (0:30), "Level 1" in *Buying XP* (0:32), the definitions in *The math behind crazy profit margins* (0:31), and "Mistake 1" in *Top 5 mistakes* (0:30).

**Open loops are visual and specific, not teased.** His VSL hook puts promise and proof in one line ("a million views or you don't pay"), then adds one loop: "1 million views = how much revenue?" ([$10M VSL 0:00–0:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_3-WQstsN5I&t=0s)). He doesn't use "stay till the end for the secret."

**Josh hook template (about 20 seconds):**
> [Thesis in one blunt line.] [The number or deal that proves it, with an on-screen disclaimer.] [What this costs people who get it wrong.] [What this video gives you: "I'll show you the X steps / every level / the exact structure."] → straight into point 1.

Example:
> "Most people trying to buy a business are trying to find the money. That's the wrong problem. In most deals I look at, the money is already sitting in the business and in the seller. [JOSH: one deal and its structure]. Here's every way to fund a deal, from the bank to the seller, and which one to use first."

## 3. The six video shapes

### A. The ladder (levels / stages)
Seen in *Every revenue level from $10k to $5M* (51 min), *Buying XP in real life* (Level 1 → 2 → 3), the awareness spectrum in *$100K/m VS $1M/m offers* (4:04–8:06), the content continuum in *How I scaled to $1m/mo in adspend* (1:33), and *$0 to $500K in 90 days* (months 1–3).

1. Hook: the top of the ladder plus a low point. Say "most people are stuck on rung 2."
2. Rung 1: what it looks like, his real numbers at that rung, the trap there, and the one move that unlocks the next rung.
3. Repeat for each rung. Each rung adds one new constraint, never a list.
4. The general rule behind the whole ladder (e.g. "each jump came from one outside insight", [Every revenue level 23:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AmXrrw8OGa0&t=1400s)).
5. Where the viewer probably is, and the next rung to work on.
6. Quiet pitch.

### B. N mistakes / N frameworks (ranked list)
Seen in *Top 5 mistakes when hitting $1M / month*, *$10k/mo vs $1m/mo paid ads* (ten mistakes), *5 Frameworks That Will Make You Too Much Money* (ranked by preference, least favourite last), *I make $3m/mo. Here are 5 business lessons*, and *Brutally Honest Advice*.

1. Hook: context number (e.g. recorded during a $6M month).
2. Each item, about 60–120 seconds:
   - name it in a few words
   - say what it cost him, as a number
   - say why it happens (the mechanism)
   - give the fix as a rule
3. Rank the items or say which matters most ("my least favourite lever", [5 Frameworks 2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d-e18KypwBQ&t=153s)).
4. End on the most counterintuitive item. *Top 5 mistakes* ends on over-optimising a working machine (8:08–9:39).

### C. Confession → diagnosis → rule (story)
Seen in *How I went broke trying to get rich... and then got rich.*, *Why I disappeared...*, *I failed...*, *I just made 5 million dollars in one month.*, *The disappointment of making $5 million per month*, and *Got fit*.

1. Hook: the milestone or the failure, stated flatly.
2. Timeline in numbers, month by month (e.g. $120K → $350K → $500K → $700K topline while CAC ran at about 150% of revenue, [Went broke 7:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XwERThzQQBU&t=466s)).
3. The moment he found the real cause, usually small and specific: a client who had never seen an ad ([Went broke 10:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XwERThzQQBU&t=622s)), or making refunds go through a recorded call instead of email ([Disappointment 1:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-l4SKfxstC4&t=94s)).
4. What he changed, and the numbers afterwards.
5. The rule for the viewer, plus where it doesn't apply ("It's not going to be one small change that fixes your business, even though it was one small change for mine", [Disappointment 1:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-l4SKfxstC4&t=94s)).
6. Ownership line ("outside of your control… not outside of your responsibility", [I failed 9:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aUgSDt1Kp4g&t=559s)).

**Key discipline:** he scrapped his first cut of *Got fit* because it was all about him, and re-filmed it around what the viewer learns ([Got fit 0:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9cHZkESVPo&t=31s)). Every story beat has to earn a lesson.

### D. X vs Y (comparison with a verdict)
Seen in *Organic content vs paid ads*, *Selling DFY vs DIY*, *$100K/m VS $1M/m offers*, and *VSL's are dead* (VSL vs text page).

1. Hook: establish that he has done both at scale (millions of organic views and $1M+/month in ads).
2. Bust the common belief ("good at organic → should run ads" is false, cost him about $500K, [Organic vs paid 3:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hk0k66Pc5Dk&t=184s)).
3. Explain what each option actually costs and what it gets you (time vs money, trust vs control, margin vs scale).
4. **A readiness test** the viewer can run on themselves ("Can you sell your product or service to a stranger on the street?", [4:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hk0k66Pc5Dk&t=246s)).
5. Verdict: usually "both, in this order", or "Y once you pass the test."
6. Hedge: "test it with your own money" ([VSL's are dead 3:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AATxUdXv4tI&t=183s)).

### E. Teardown of a real asset ("copy it")
Seen in *This landing page makes me $116,000 per day*, *These emails made me $720K*, *How to create a $10,000,000 VSL*, *Every number I tracked to hit $3m/mo*, *8 questions improved my business by 2,629%*, and the live roasts (*I Roasted a 6-Figure Business Owner*, *Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger*).

1. Hook: the asset and what it produced.
2. Walk through it **in the order the prospect experiences it** ([$116K/day landing page](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w5bYv2CBDZE), from traffic to call), or field by field or column by column.
3. For each part, cover:
   - what it is
   - the reason it exists (usually an objection or a leak)
   - the number attached to it (e.g. revenue per call for each application answer in *8 questions*)
4. Show what's been tried and dropped ("10M views" tested worse than "1M").
5. "Every line in here was once said on a sales call that made us money" ([$10M VSL 4:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_3-WQstsN5I&t=248s)): show where each part came from.
6. Tell the viewer to copy it, then say why copying alone won't be enough (*A message to my competitors...*).

### F. The short contrarian talk (4–9 min)
Seen in *Being "smart" is killing your business*, *agencies suck*, *Why do you turn the music down…*, *Hustle culture is broken*, and *These 6 minutes…*.

1. Contrarian claim.
2. Why people believe the opposite.
3. One analogy or anecdote from outside business (North Slope paint inspectors earning 3–4x the painters, [Being smart 2:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tCNqyltvwPI&t=125s)).
4. A test the viewer can apply ("explain it to a 10-year-old", 4:37).
5. Closing line he could post as a tweet.

**For The 3C Machine:** use A, B, D and E for teaching episodes and C for proof and origin episodes. Use F for Shorts and cut-downs.

## 4. How he explains a concept

His usual order for one idea, as in *I paid 20k to learn this in 5 minutes*:

1. **The rule in one line.** "You want to sell a result, not just a subscription." (0:00)
2. **One analogy from everyday life.** Nobody keeps paying for a book they've already read (0:30). A personal trainer: clients get most of the value early (1:32).
3. **The number that makes it real.** People pay $10K once more readily than $1K a month for 24 months (3:03).
4. **The mechanism, as math.** SaaS at $100/mo × 24 months = $2,400 LTV, so it takes about 10 months to earn back each customer's acquisition cost (3:33).
5. **His own P&L as proof.** $1.3M → $3M a month.
6. **The objection, raised and answered.** "I'll get fewer clients" is actually an advantage (6:35).
7. **The decision rule.** Double the price, half the clients, same revenue, half the delivery cost (7:06).

**Analogies come from everyday life and stay short:**
- Pokémon XP ([Buying XP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=segcbjg2cGs))
- a tree bearing fruit (partnerships)
- Planet Fitness cancellations (refunds)
- the burning house vs the dream home ([Brutally Honest 0:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VhlBAF8isF8&t=30s))
- a polluting factory taxed by Facebook
- a towel used two ways (ad angles)
- learning to ride a bike (paying for experience)
- a car dealership (a good product survives a bad sales process)

He uses one analogy per idea and moves on.

**Numbers:**
- Use exact, odd numbers: 2,671 leads, a 39% disqualification rate, 1.8% close rate, $400 in the bank, 2,629%.
- Give before and after: refunds went from about 40% to about 5%.
- Use ratios: ad spend as 24% of revenue; 60 / 27 / 13 staff split.
- Work back from the goal: clients → calls → conversations → leads → daily budget ([$0 to $500K 0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AZC82IKCiVI&t=0s)).
- Use the average, not the best case, as proof ([$10M VSL 5:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_3-WQstsN5I&t=340s)).

**Nuance is part of the brand.** He states the rule hard, then says where it stops working:
- "VSLs still work… test it with your own money" ([VSL's are dead 3:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AATxUdXv4tI&t=183s))
- richer leads aren't always better ([8 questions 6:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkCQwWRMlIs&t=371s))
- "Don't even listen to me. Measure your own data." ([8 questions 17:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkCQwWRMlIs&t=1020s))
- both options are valid, pick deliberately ([Stranger 11:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eWMz7IfiHbE&t=714s))

When he says "it depends", he always names what it depends on (budget, traffic temperature, deal cycle) and gives a test.

**Tone:**
- He speaks as an accountant-turned-operator: "I was going to… moneyball the whole thing" ([Every number 0:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jgkuFYLRLnA&t=31s)).
- He is dry and unhyped, and sometimes says "dude" ("It's just posting videos, dude", [Hustle 6:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ppgr1dhJzAM&t=371s)).
- He deliberately filters out beginners: longer, slower content for owners who have already built something, and fewer views from the right people ([Why I don't get views 2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LjswhfXCmCo&t=153s)).
- He owns his losses openly.
- He doesn't use superlatives, urgency theatre or "secret" framing, apart from the occasional title.

## 5. Pacing and density

- **Sentences:** short and spoken, one idea each. Aim for a 6th-grade reading level or lower; he says his biggest videos average 3rd grade (playbook B2.8, [$36K training 1:44:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hp328yUV6O8&t=6255s)). Cut preamble.
- **Speed:** a new teaching point roughly every 30–60 seconds in the short videos. The notes show a new nugget at nearly every 30-second mark in his 5–12 minute videos.
- **Length:** match the shape:
  - talks: 4–9 min
  - ranked lists and teardowns: 10–20 min
  - "complete map" breakdowns: 30–90 min
  - full trainings: up to 2h39

  Put the length in the title when it's long ("51 minute breakdown").
- **Transitions** are labels, not segues: "Mistake 2…", "Level 3…", "The second thing…", "Here's where it breaks." In ad mechanisms he uses "First, Next, Then" (playbook B2.3).
- **Recaps:**
  - In milestone videos, he recaps the earlier unlock before the new one ([I just made 5M 2:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jm14bVak6rA&t=154s)).
  - In ladders, the closing rule is the recap.
  - In VSLs, he recaps with the CTA at 45–60 seconds.
- **Critical information comes in the first 2.5–3 minutes.** That's when social-traffic attention drops. Then add about 2 minutes for skeptics: budget, authority, need and timing objections, compared with the alternatives (playbook C2.5).
- **The pitch is late, short and narrow.**
  - In *I just made 5M* the partnership offer comes at 7:10 of 8 minutes.
  - In *The disappointment* it comes at 5:48, framed as who he will and won't work with.
  - In *I'll invest in your business*, the CTA is a short application, the founders call personally, and they only take you on if confident they can change your business (9:41).
  - Non-fits get a free option: a Skool group ([Organic vs paid 11:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hk0k66Pc5Dk&t=705s)) or free training (playbook C2.14).
  - He says who it's *not* for, more than once ([$10M VSL 2:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_3-WQstsN5I&t=124s)).

  The 3C Machine wants a soft pitch at about 2:30 plus a full close. Keep the 2:30 mention to one or two sentences in Daniel's register ("This is what we do with people in the Academy; more at the end") and save the full offer for the end.

## 6. Do / don't for writers

**Do**
- Put the thesis and its proving number in the first 15 seconds.
- Use a title number that's specific and odd-looking (1.8%, 2,629%, $116,000), with a disclaimer if it's a result.
- Pick one shape (A–F) per episode and label each section out loud ("Rung 2", "Mistake 3").
- Explain every idea in order: one-line rule → one everyday analogy → one number → mechanism → decision rule.
- Show the math on screen: a worked deal with round numbers, labelled illustrative.
- Give a self-test ("If you can't answer X, you're not ready for Y").
- State what the rule depends on, and say plainly where it doesn't apply.
- Tell the low point as plainly as the win. Use `[JOSH: …]` for any real detail not in the source.
- Write for operators. Assume the viewer is smart and short on time.
- Pitch narrowly at the end: who it's for, who it isn't, a short application, and a free option for non-fits.

**Don't**
- Open with "Hey guys", a channel intro or a subscribe ask.
- Tease ("stay till the end for the secret") or hold the recipe back.
- Use hype adjectives, urgency theatre, lifestyle flexing or exclamation marks. Sultanic devices are fine; Daniel would deliver them flatly.
- Stack several analogies on one point, or use analogies that need explaining.
- Use vague numbers ("a lot of money", "tons of deals") or the best case without the average.
- Make a story about the presenter without a lesson for the viewer (the *Got fit* rule).
- Pretend there's one universal answer. He says "test it" and "measure your own data."
- Write for beginners with definitions-only content. Define a term in one line and move on.
- Pitch more than once mid-video, or pitch to everyone.
- Invent Daniel quotes, numbers or timestamps. Use only what's in `playbook.md` and `video-notes.md`.
