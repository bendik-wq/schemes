**The recipe, fast (0:45–2:30)**

Three things change as a deal gets bigger.

One. More people can say no. At $3 million, it's mostly you, the seller and maybe one lender. At $15 million there's often a credit committee, a bigger accounting firm doing the quality-of-earnings work, lawyers on both sides, sometimes a landlord, sometimes the seller's family. Every one of them is a door that can close.

Two. The checks get harder, not easier. Bigger deals often get better terms. On larger deals, roughly over $5 million, banks often don't need a personal guarantee, because the business's assets are their security. Typically. Every lender and deal is different. But the price of that is scrutiny. Every number gets tested.

[SCREEN: "Typically; every lender and deal is different. Education, not financial or legal advice."]

Three. Time is a risk all on its own. The longer the process, the more chances for a bad month, a nervous partner, or a seller who changes their mind.

So the question for any big deal is: which of those doors is most likely to close, and what are you doing about it this week?

**Why they say yes in the first place (3:00)**

A lot of owners in traditional industries are ready to retire. Their problem isn't price. It's the burden of owning the thing. They don't want to wait 16 weeks for a bank. They want out, and they want to be looked after.
