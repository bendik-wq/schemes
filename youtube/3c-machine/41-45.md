Two. Some things get easier and the checks get harder. On larger deals, roughly over $5 million, banks often don't need a personal guarantee, because the business's assets are their security. Typically. Every lender and deal is different. But the price of that is scrutiny. Every number gets tested.

[SCREEN: "Typically; every lender and deal is different. Education, not financial or legal advice."]

Red flag: your board is friends and family. If they've no relevant deals or exits, a bank and a seller can tell. Fix it before you make offers.

**Capital (3:50)**

[SCREEN: "Capital" lit]

Next, the money.

Red flag: the deal doesn't clear 1.5. Free cash flow covers all repayments less than one and a half times. That's not a deal. That's a slow month away from trouble.

Red flag: no working capital line approved before settlement. If fees and runway depend on something the bank hasn't signed, you can close and run out of cash in the same week.

**Closing: the offer and diligence (5:40)**

[SCREEN: "The offer · Due diligence"]

In the LOI, red flag: no conditions. Financing approval, quality-of-earnings sign-off, the lease, licences, key staff agreements and the seller's transition should all be in there. No exclusivity period is another one.

In diligence, the big ones:

One or two customers or suppliers making up more than about 30% of revenue. Over half, and you walk or heavily discount.

Gross margin slipping, hidden by accounting changes.

Tax debt or liens.

Key customer contracts that can't be assigned to a new owner.

Licences or compliance problems in a regulated industry.

Very high staff turnover.

And a seller who wants to leave the day you settle, with no transition. That's high severity. Walk.
