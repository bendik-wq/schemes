## How Sultanic actually writes long posts on X

This is from analysis of about 40 of his real posts, 21 of them long-form.

**Shape of a long post**
1. **Headline line.** Title Case, often with an em-dash or a bracketed qualifier. Example: "The #1 Reason Owners Never Buy Their Competitor (and it isn't money)". Write it last.
2. **A letter-like opener.** Either a casual greeting on its own line ("Morning,") followed by a present-moment scene, or straight into a story mid-scene, with dialogue: "Had a call yesterday with an owner doing $3M a year. He goes, 'Josh, I'd love to buy the bloke down the road, but I haven't got $2M sitting around.'" Use the greeting on about half the posts, not all of them.
3. **The conventional view, then reject it bluntly.** "Everyone tells you you need a deposit. They're wrong."
4. **Mock the vague version of the advice before giving the real how.** "'Just grow by acquisition,' they say. Great. How? ...crickets."
5. **Teach one real framework.** Numbered steps where they fit. Give real value; the post must be useful even if nobody clicks.
6. **Prove possibility in three layers, in this order:**
   - Possible in the world.
   - Possible for others like them.
   - Possible for them.

   Sultanic: "'WHAT'S POSSIBLE' creates space for 'HOW IT'S POSSIBLE'."
7. **Create desire through lack, by comparison.** Show what owners who buy have that the reader doesn't: the group, the exit multiple, the weeks off.
8. **Use one identity bind.** "Here's why smart owners buy their competitor instead of out-advertising them."
9. **Use inner monologue.** Voice the reader's objections as an ellipsis-led stack: "...But where does the money come from? ...What if the bank says no? ...Do I even have the time?" Then answer each one as a statement, not a question. That's the assumptive rule: "And the reason the bank says yes is...".
10. **Use the Not-Your-Fault frame once.** "It has nothing to do with your [x] or [y]. It has everything to do with [z]."
11. **Pitch late and soft.** One or two sentences near the end. "That's what we do with owners in 90 days..."
12. **Sign off like a letter.** One of: "Talk soon," / "To your next deal," / "Have a great week,". Then "Josh" on its own line.
13. **P.S. carries the link.** "P.S. If your business does $1M+ a year, answer 10 questions and see if you qualify. Takes two minutes: apply.grosfjeld-li.com". Then the disclaimer line as the very last line.
14. **Optional P.P.S.** Use it for an engagement ask ("Drop a comment with your industry and I'll tell you what I'd buy first.") or a tease of a related idea. Use it on about a third of posts.

**Rhythm and spacing**
- Every paragraph is separated by a blank line.
- Median paragraph is about 15–20 words. Mix spoken, run-on sentences with one-line punches.
- Ellipses: about 1 in 10 paragraphs, used in bursts to carry one thought across lines. Never on every line.
- Lists are numbered, short, and used for the framework only.
- No emoji, no hashtags, no thread numbering, no bold, no "⸻" dividers, no markdown.

**Length:** 450–900 words per post. Longer than the current versions, but every paragraph must earn its place.

**Josh's signature words.** Use these sparingly, and not all in one post:
- "the acquisition game"
- "Here's why"
- "See how that works?"
- "the bloke down the road"

Don't copy Sultanic's own signatures ("food for thought, you do the dishes", "the almighty algo", "Magick", NHB/FF references).

## Frameworks from the skill to rotate across your 10 posts

Each post opens with **one** opener type. Use at least five different ones across your ten.

| Opener | Use for |
|---|---|
| Two-Types | "There are two types of owners..." |
| How Come | "How come owners with a team, a track record and profit still never buy anything?" |
| From-To | "Running a $1M business is great... until..." |
| Secret Keeper | "There's a well-kept secret among owners who grow fast..." |
| Saturated Mechanism | The ads/hiring/grind treadmill, then the new way |
| Chad vs James | Two owners side by side |
| Story or scene | Mid-conversation |
| What You Were Told Was Lying | A belief-shift opener |
| Market Has Moved | The retiring-owner wave |

Each post also uses:
- One belief shift: What You Were Told Was Lying / That's Not How It Works / Before I Knew–After I Knew / Proof That Changes The Story / Market Has Moved.
- One identity line: You Already Know This / This Is Who You Are Now / Permission Slip / Threshold Moment.
- A close built from Two Paths or No Choice, plus the 30-days After Close ("30 days from now you'll be 30 days older either way...") and/or an Identity Close ("If you're someone who...").

Use the After Close in only about half of the posts so it doesn't feel templated.

## Hard rules

1. **No new facts.** Every number, person and result must already be in your original post or in `source-owners-academy.md`. Keep the relationships exact:
   - Cam, Jason Nagy and Elliot are friends.
   - Jy Kingston is a student.
   - Saul is an Owners Academy member.
   - Christian Dawes is an example, not a client.

   Scenes like "Had a call yesterday with an owner..." must stay generic and clearly typical ("I talk to owners every week who say..."), never a fake specific person or event.
2. **Keep each post's disclaimer line** as the very last line, unchanged in meaning. Add "Not financial advice." where structure, tax or legal points appear.
3. **No price, no fake scarcity or urgency, no income promises, no guarantees** beyond the real 7-day money-back guarantee.
4. **No exclamation marks, no profanity**, no Jesus close, no Good Karma close.
5. **Keep Josh's voice.** Australian, direct, calm, a bit blunt, plain English. Sultanic's structure, Josh's mouth. Not hype.
6. **Keep the post number and topic.** Write a new Title-Case headline as the body's first line. The `# N. <Title>` heading can change to a sharper title of the same topic.
7. **The link appears once,** inside the P.S. line, written exactly `apply.grosfjeld-li.com`.
