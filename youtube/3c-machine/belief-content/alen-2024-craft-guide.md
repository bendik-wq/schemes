# Alen Sultanic 2024: Belief-Shift Craft Guide

Source: "Alen-1.txt" (251 dated posts, Jan 2 – Dec 31, 2024), read in full. Everything in quotation marks is verbatim from the file, typos included, and has its date heading; "[...]" marks a cut. Anything labelled **[OURS]** is our adaptation for Josh Li / G&L M&A Advisory. It is not his.

## 1. How he shifts a belief: the recurring move sequences

**1. "X vs real X" redefinition.** Steps: take a word the reader thinks they own → split it into a fake version and a real one → define the real one by *behaviour* → show the reader is in the fake one → binary question.
- Jan 2: "There are decisions, and then there are real decisions." → "So if you're still "thinking" about it, you haven't decided [...] These are just mere decisions. They're not real decisions." → "Have you made a decision, or have you made a real decision?"

**2. Name the fear, then show "how it really works" with worst-case math.** Steps: state the common belief and the fear under it → partly concede it → run the numbers at their worst → recast the loss as buying something → finish on the asymmetry.
- Jan 12: "So you think that you're "losing" money. All fear come from loss, and here's how it really works." → "So your downside is a loss of $10 a day and your upside is millions."

**3. The Flip Over.** Steps: find the one element that sets the frame (a word, an order, who pays whom) → invert it → show how everything downstream changes.
- Feb 29 ("The Free Trial Flip Over"): "This takes the thought of FREE as a forethought and puts it as an afterthought".
- Mar 12: "The flip over in the sales process is to showcase how that $5k gives permission".
- Apr 21: "going from "they pay me" to "they pay me to"."

**4. The Mirror Frame.** Steps: learn *their* economics → set your price against one unit of their result → the deal pays for itself → say the reversal out loud.
- Feb 22: "Here is my price , and that's just ONE person joining the____ program at $8.5k, that's it. I help you get one person, and it's break even" → "Notice — now, they're not paying YOU, you're paying yourself, using their process." → "It's not how much they pay you, but how much they make that justifies what you make in the process."

**5. Loss → Gain conversion.** Steps: fear comes from loss → name the specific loss → stack the gains from it, or find a way to keep both.
- Jan 17: "What causes fear is loss, so look for what loss is taking place. Once you find what loss is taking place, turn that into gain".
- Nov 15: "What do I lose when I move forward?" → "1. What do I gain out of losing this? [...] 2. What can I do differently so I don't lose that and have both?"

**6. Lack → Abundance hierarchy.** Steps: list what they have plenty of and what they lack → remove the lack first → close with what they have plenty of as the reason they're "a perfect fit".
- Apr 13: "We value what we lack (don't have) and don't value what we have in abundance (what we do have)." → "You lead with lack and you overcome them head on and you end with abundance that they have which makes them a perfect fit." → "with B2B, the thing they're generally abundant in is money, but lack time, so you just flip it around".

**7. Closed-door syllogism.** Steps: if they could, they would have → they want to (they showed intent) → so the gap is "how" → "how" needs you.
- Feb 26: "If you could do it, you would have done it, and since you didn't do it, you can't do it".
- May 17: "So the only logical conclusion left is that the problem they have is not the real problem, hence why they're stuck."

**8. Equation chain.** Steps: compress the belief into an "=" or "→" chain that reads as a law → invert it to give the prescription.
- Jan 23: "Low status = high risk = high guarantee to offset the risk. High status = low risk = no guarantee to offset the risk."
- Aug 23: "High output (outcome) / low input = market message match".

**9. Relativity reframe.** Steps: refuse their comparison → reveal what is really being bought → set a new comparison.
- Jun 4: "Curious, expensive compared to what?" → "all objection handling is done via the modification of relativity between two objects of value."
- Sep 21: "The thing is, when you buy a water bottle, you're not paying for the water itself."

**10. Stretch, then compress the timeline.** Steps: stretch time until "impossible" becomes obvious → pull it back to now → ask what shortens it.
- May 2: "on a long enough timeline anything is possible" → "How do I shorten that timeline so that it's possible now?"

**11. Expose the omission.** Point to the missing part of the common pitch; behaviour beats words.
- Jan 17: "Pay attention to what they're not telling you and you'll see how they make their money."
- Feb 28: "Do as they do, not as they say".

**12. Check-in, then self-diagnosis close.** After a worked example he asks whether it landed: "See how that works?" (Mar 28); "See how easy that was?" (May 17). Then he ends on a question the reader answers about themselves: "Is your sales team pushing or pulling? Is your sales team pitching or presenting?" (Mar 26); "Are they "paying you" or are they "paying you to"? Food for thought." (Apr 21)

1. "decisions" vs "real decisions" (Jan 2)
2. "You're never walking away from something or someone. You're always walking towards something or someone else that's better for you." (Jan 11)
3. "Most are looking to find their “why” when in reality they need to find their “who” first." (Jan 20)

7. "Is your sales team pushing or pulling? Is your sales team pitching or presenting?" (Mar 26)
8. "The first is what they don't want to copy. The second is what they can't copy." (Mar 27)
9. "Are they USING It or are they CONSUMING what you provide for them? BIG difference." (Apr 18)
10. ""they pay me" to "they pay me to"" (Apr 21)
11. "Advice WITH consequences = strategic moves. Advice WITHOUT consequences = foolish moves." (May 7)
12. "Bad copy makes it so that you want something FROM them, good copy makes it so you want something FOR them." (May 20)
13. "focusing on decision extensions rather than decisions themselves" (Sep 19)
14. "Control is an illusion, choice is the reality." (Oct 13)
15. "you're either leverage or have leverage." (Nov 18)

**How the device works:** he names both sides, gives the reader's current side the smaller or more passive verb (using/consuming, pitch/present, saving/making), and closes with "BIG difference", "The difference that makes all the difference." (May 20), or a self-check question.

## 5. Rhythm and structure of a typical post

- **Length:** median about 190 words; a quarter are under 100 (aphorisms); about 10% are over 700; the longest is about 3,900 ("The Guide To Raising Prices", Nov 26). Mostly short posts, with a deep long-form post every couple of weeks.
- **Paragraphs:** one to three lines each. He states the rule himself: "No more than 4 lines of text per text block." (Feb 24); its p.s.: "What you'll notice is that all of my posts and emails follow this principle."
- **Ellipsis chains:** about 370 lines end with "..." and about 290 start with "...". The thought trails off and the next line picks it up: "...But there's a fork in the road that determines if you'll really do it versus looking back a year from now wishing that you did it." (Jan 2)
- **Questions:** about 190 lines end in "?". Three kinds: a set-up ("Why?" alone on a line, Mar 14), Socratic dismantling ("But who are these others? where do they come from? and where do they exist?", Feb 15), and a self-diagnosis close.
- **Architecture:** claim or common view → credential or case ("Had the biggest golf company in the game come to me a year ago for consulting", Feb 10) → mechanism or reframe → numbers or worked example → "See how that works?" → numbered process → self-check question → sign-off → P.S.
- **Tics:** "Nothing more, nothing less." (12×), "That's it.", "Simple as that.", "and all that fun stuff", "Here's the million dollar answer:" (Oct 29).
- **Sign-offs (verbatim):** "- Alen"; "Food for thought. You do the dishes." (Jan 3); "Food for thought, you do the dishes." (Feb 27); "Food for thought," (Mar 14); "To your success," (13×); "All the best," (11×); "Have a great weekend,"; "Good morning and enjoy your coffee," (Mar 14); "Good night," (Feb 29); "'till next time," (Apr 1); "All for love of the game," (Jun 15). Long posts often open with "Morning," (18×).
- **P.S. (41 posts), five jobs:**
  - the deeper layer ("p.s. Remember, there are good decisions, bad decisions, ok decisions, all kinds of decisions, but there are no wrong decisions", Jan 2);
  - a caveat ("P.S. This graphic did not 3x the conversions by itself", Jun 19);
  - a tease ("p.s. The second biggest secret to scaling a business or an offer in any market is the concept of "scaling in the negative", which I'll share at another time.", Feb 27);
  - meta-proof (Dec 28, above);
  - a confession ("P.S. For a good part of my journey in the game, I thought that I was building a real business, but instead I was just creating promotions.", Jun 20).

## 6. 25 transferable moves for acquisition-curious owners

Each line gives the move, then **[OURS]** for an example adapted to owners doing $1M–$20M a year.

1. **Decision vs real decision.** [OURS] "There's 'thinking about buying a competitor', and then there's a signed LOI."
2. **Fear → worst-case math.** [OURS] "You picture a seven-figure cheque. Here's how the money actually moves: bank debt, a seller note, seller rollover, and the deal has to clear 1.5× DSCR first."
3. **Lack → abundance.** [OURS] Remove what they lack (deal flow, structure, time). Close on what they have plenty of (operating know-how, cash flow, a team) as why they're the ideal buyer.
4. **Mirror frame.** [OURS] "At 1.5× DSCR the target's own cash flow covers the debt with a cushion. The business pays for itself."
5. **Flip who carries the risk.** [OURS] "When the seller carries a note and rolls equity, they're betting on you."
6. **Decision extension.** [OURS] "You already decided to grow. This is the fastest way to carry out that decision, not a new one."
7. **The problem isn't the problem.** [OURS] "Capital isn't what's stopping you. Nobody has shown you a deal paid for by the business being bought."
8. **Now problem, not later problem.** [OURS] Sell capacity, customers and a team this quarter, not succession planning someday.
9. **Safety before freedom.** [OURS] "Your business keeps running exactly as it is; the acquisition sits beside it."
10. **Older sellers: change back to the same.** [OURS] "Your people, your name, your customers stay looked after. This is how you protect them."
11. **Two-way commitment, not a guarantee.** [OURS] "We run the search, model the deal and line up the financing; you commit to the calls, the documents and a decision in the window."
12. **Compared to what?** [OURS] "Expensive compared to building the same revenue from scratch over five years?"
13. **Bottled water.** [OURS] "You're not buying assets; you're buying customers who already pay, staff who already show up, and cash flow already in the bank."
14. **Status over guarantees.** [OURS] Show deal history and closed structures instead of promising outcomes.
15. **Loss → gain questions.** [OURS] "What do you lose if you buy? What do you gain by losing it? How could you keep both?"
16. **Stretch, then compress.** [OURS] "Organic growth gets you there on a long enough timeline. Buying is how you shorten it."
17. **Short-term language.** [OURS] "In the next 90 days", not "Year 1, Year 2, Year 3". He warns: "DO NOT mark a timeline beyond 14-21 days" (Jul 9).
18. **Towards language.** [OURS] "Add a location, double capacity," not "stop being stuck." These owners are builders.
19. **Already paid the price.** [OURS] Target owners who have already opened a second site or hired a GM.
20. **Permission holders.** [OURS] "The bank's approval and the seller's signature are the permission slips; our job is getting you both."
21. **"Do you think you can…?"** [OURS] "Do you think you could look seriously at one competitor this quarter?"
22. **The word "different".** [OURS] "What $5M owners are doing differently to buy competitors without writing the cheque."
23. **Show the money.** [OURS] Lead with terms, DSCR, debt service and take-home, never headline revenue.
24. **Who isn't paying you yet?** [OURS] "An acquisition hands you customers your marketing was never going to reach."
25. **Be leverage or have leverage.** [OURS] "You've been the leverage in your business. Owning a second one is how you start having it."

## 7. Things to avoid copying

- **Brand phrases:** "the game", "Dark lord tip of the day:" (Oct 10), "Food for thought, you do the dishes.", "To your success", "copy frens", "bro", "$$$", "GiVmE aN eXaMpLe!!!" (Mar 9), "kick up the algo".
- **His community and products:** NHB, NHB+, Fast Forward / FF, Automatic Clients / AC, 2 Hour Agency, "hot seats". They point at his brand, not ours.
- **Profanity** (about 38 lines), e.g. "SIX FUCKING CENTS PER LEAD!" (Jan 13), "See the fuckery" (Jan 22), "A fuck all phrase" (Dec 4).
- **Mystical register:** "The Power Of The Orgasm Wish" (Sep 29); "We don't live in a simulation. We live in an imagination." (Aug 7); "This is one of the processes of real manifestation." (Oct 16); "Intentionally change someone's life and the universe will intentionally give you money." (Sep 5); the God/creator posts (Jul 31, Dec 2).
- **Ego flexing:** "My mind > their mind." (Feb 15); "I charge $5,000 / hour for consulting" (Sep 13). Keep the credential-by-case structure and drop the brag.
- **Grey-hat tactics:** junk-click sabotage (Oct 10), "trustworthy names" for pen names (Dec 23), celeb-name press releases (Oct 4), "Go hard AF in some" geo-compliance (Jul 6).
- **Guru-sniping and talking down to the reader.** "If you could do it, you would have done it" (Feb 26) must be softened for owners, per his own "customer service perspective" rule.
- **Critical blind spot for our offer:** his readers already hold these associations, and our audience may too.
  - "No money down" is, in his words, "Every 80s and 90s real estate infomercial." (Apr 13).
  - He warns that the "deal maker" will 'usually come from or have "experience" in other "deal worlds", such as real estate, mergers and acquisitions and "buying businesses", but never with their own hard work, creativity or money.' (Nov 28).
  - He mocks "owning a portfolio of businesses" (Dec 4).
  - Pre-empt that suspicion with mechanics (DSCR, debt service, seller-note terms), the buyer's control of the money ("whoever controls the money controls the business", Oct 25 P.S.), and real deal substance. Never lead with the phrase on its own.
