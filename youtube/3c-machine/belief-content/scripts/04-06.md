When you walk into a bank and ask for money for you, the bank looks at you. Your income, your assets, your credit file, what it can take if things go wrong. That's borrowing. You're the thing being assessed, and the house is the obvious security, because you're the obvious risk.

Now the money. Say the bank funds part of the price. The seller agrees to be paid the rest over time. And the bank adds a working-capital line on top, so the business has room to breathe from the first week.

Keep it illustrative. The bank's piece might cost around a hundred and seventy thousand a year to repay, and the seller's piece around a hundred thousand. That's roughly two hundred and seventy thousand going out, against five hundred thousand coming in...

To be blunt, though, no personal guarantee doesn't mean no risk. The risk doesn't disappear. It moves into the debt and the seller's terms, and that's exactly where you want it, because that's where you can measure it.
