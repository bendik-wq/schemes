Let's start with the belief itself, because it sounds sensible. "I'll do it when the timing is right." Nobody argues with that sentence. It sounds patient. It sounds like the sort of thing a careful owner says over a beer at the local.

That's exactly what the ratio is. A weight limit for debt, set by the business itself, with room left over.

Now, if rates go up, what changes? The structure changes. More of the price gets paid to the seller over time, the term gets longer, the split between the bank and the seller moves. What doesn't change is the ratio. One and a half times is one and a half times in any year.

So there's a good time to buy, and then there's a good deal to buy. A deal that clears one and a half times is a good deal in a rough market. A deal that doesn't clear it is a bad deal in the best market you'll ever see.
