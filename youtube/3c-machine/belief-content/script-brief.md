# Brief: 12 long-form belief-shift YouTube scripts for Josh Li

You're writing long-form YouTube scripts (about 17–20 minutes each) that Josh Li reads to camera. Each script dismantles one belief that stops owners of $1M–$20M businesses from buying a competitor or a retiring owner's business, and installs a new one in its place. The user loves how Alen Sultanic **writes and decompiles subjects**. That is the soul of these scripts: one subject, taken apart into its pieces, one distinction at a time, so fluidly that each line pulls you into the next.

## Read first, fully

1. `youtube/3c-machine/belief-content/belief-map.md` gives your belief rows: the old belief, the new belief, the distinction, the allowed proof and the identity line.
2. `youtube/3c-machine/belief-content/alen-2024-craft-guide.md` covers how Sultanic shifts beliefs: move patterns, distinctions, rhythm and the B2B principles. **This is your main style source.**
3. `youtube/3c-machine/belief-content/hypnotic-devices-b2b.md`. Use 4–6 of these devices per script, by its checklist, and none from its do-not-use list.
4. `youtube/3c-machine/source-owners-academy.md` is the only source for Josh's facts and proof.
5. `youtube/3c-machine/x-posts/sultanic-x-brief.md`, sections "What the landing page sells" and "Hard rules", which still apply.

## What each script must do

**Decompile the subject, Alen-style.** Take the belief and break it into its parts. "There's growth, and then there's real growth." "There are three pieces to this, and most owners only ever look at one." Walk each piece in turn, slowly, building. Use his moves:

- **Name the common view, partly concede it, then show how it really works.** "And that's true, as far as it goes... but here's what's actually happening."
- **X vs real X distinctions.** Every script is built on at least one central distinction, plus two or three smaller ones along the way.
- **The Flip Over.** Swap the one element that sets the frame, so the whole picture turns over: the seller funds you instead of the bank, or the price becomes a payment plan.
- **Worst-case numbers, then a small downside against a big upside.** Make the risk concrete and bounded before you show the prize.
- **"See how that works?"** after an example, used sparingly: no more than twice per script.
- **End sections on a question the viewer answers about themselves.**
- **Lack to abundance for B2B.** Owners have money and lack time, so lead by removing the time burden and close on what they already have plenty of: the team, the track record, the profit, the industry knowledge.
- **The Mirror Frame where it fits.** Set the cost or effort against one unit of the viewer's own result.
- **Present the big step as an extension of a decision they've already made,** for example "you already decided to build something that lasts...".
- **Towards language and short timelines.** Talk about the first conversation this month, not "Month 1, 2, 3".

**Meet the scepticism head-on.** Sophisticated owners associate "no money down" with 80s and 90s infomercials and with "deal makers" who never put in their own money. Earn trust with mechanics:

- debt service, the 1.5× ratio
- seller-note terms
- where the money actually comes from
- what the downside is
- "what are the terms? what was the take-home?"

Never flex income.

**Flow.** It must read as one continuous thought, spoken:

- Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences.
- Ellipses carry a thought across lines, in bursts, roughly every 8–10 paragraphs, never on every line.
- Lists are spoken, not formatted: "The first piece is... The second piece is...".
- No headings, labels, timestamps, beat names, on-screen notes or B-roll cues. **Pure script: only what Josh says.**

## Shape of each script (a guide, not boxes)

1. **Cold open, first 30 seconds.** The belief named, its cost made concrete, and the open loop: what this video will take apart.
2. **The decompile.** The subject broken into 3–5 pieces, each with its distinction, a worked example with numbers, and the flip.
3. **Proof:**
   - **Possible in the world:** a general truth.
   - **Possible for others:** sourced proof, relationships exact.
   - **Possible for you:** the viewer's existing advantages.
4. **The cost of keeping the old belief.** Passive risk, and the comparison with owners who've already made the shift.
5. **The identity close.** Who the viewer is now that they know this.
6. **The invitation, once,** at the end, plus one short soft mention around a third of the way in. The script's last spoken sentence is the invitation:
   - "If your business does a million or more a year, the link's below. It's ten questions, about two minutes, and it gets you on a call with my team to map what your first acquisition could look like. If we can help, we'll show you how. If we can't, we'll tell you straight."
   - The 7-day refund at most once in a script, and not in every one.

## Hard rules

- **Length:** 2,400–3,000 spoken words per script.
- **Voice:** Josh's. Australian, calm, direct, operator to operator, plain English. No hype, no exclamation marks, no profanity, no mystical or manifestation language, no fake scarcity, no income promises, no price.
- **Facts:** no new ones. Every fact about Josh, his deals and other people comes from `source-owners-academy.md` or the landing-page facts in the X brief. Illustrative numbers are said to be illustrative ("say a business makes $500K..."). Keep relationships exact:
  - Cam, Jason Nagy and Elliot are friends.
  - Jy Kingston is a student.
  - Saul is an Owners Academy member; his deal is at NDA and early due diligence, not closed.
  - Christian Dawes is "an example I use, not a client".
- **Spoken disclaimers:** briefly, where results or structures appear. "Results like that aren't typical." "This is general information, not financial advice."
- **Standalone:** each script never mentions other videos, a series, episodes or "next time".
- **Numbers:** written for a teleprompter, for example "$500K" read as "five hundred thousand", "1.5×" as "one and a half times". Write numbers the way Josh would say them, either "$1.5 million" or "one and a half million", consistently within a script.
