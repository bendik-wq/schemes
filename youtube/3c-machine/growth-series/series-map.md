# The Growth Ceiling: 12 videos, one theme

**Core theme: massive growth.** Every owner of a $1M–$20M business eventually hits a ceiling. Revenue still moves, but growth turns linear, heavy and personal: one customer, one hire, one more of your own hours at a time. Organic growth adds. Buying multiplies.

Each video takes one bottleneck to massive growth and shows that buying a competitor or a retiring owner's business, with no money down and the deal structured to pay for itself, breaks that bottleneck.

**The undercurrent: the step up in status.** It's never sold overtly. It comes through scenes, contrasts and identity lines. The viewer quietly sees the level above where they are now:

- the owner of a group, not a job;
- the buyer the industry calls;
- a seat at different tables, with bankers, boards and private equity;
- the respect of peers;
- a legacy to hand on.

Each video shows that level through one or two concrete pictures, never by bragging.

**The offer every video points to.** A 10-question application at apply.grosfjeld-li.com for an acquisition strategy call with Josh's team. The promise is a signed LOI within 90 days on an acquisition that can double or triple the business. Deals are over-financed, structured at about 1.5× DSCR, and done with the owner, not for them. There is a 7-day money-back guarantee, and the price is never stated.

| # | Bottleneck | Old belief | Turn | Status picture (subtle) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The ceiling** | "We'll break through if we push harder next year." | The ceiling is structural. Organic growth adds; acquisition multiplies. | The owner whose name comes up when people talk about who's growing |
| 2 | **Linear growth** | "Growth is one more customer at a time." | Grow one business at a time. Step growth, not slope growth. | Doubling in a year while peers report another 8% |
| 3 | **Capacity** | "We can't take on more. We're at capacity." | Buy capacity that already exists: crews, vans, licences, systems. | Turning work away vs being the one who says yes to the big contract |
| 4 | **Talent** | "I can't find good people." | Buy a team that already works together. | The owner good people want to work for, because it's going somewhere |
| 5 | **The marketing treadmill** | "Growth means spending more to win customers." | Buy customers who already stay. A book of business beats a campaign. | Competitors' customers becoming yours, quietly |
| 6 | **Cash** | "Growth eats cash, so we have to slow down." | Structures where the growth funds itself: seller-funded, over-financed, 1.5×. | The bank calling you, not the other way round |
| 7 | **The owner** | "Nobody can do it like me." | The second business forces the layer that frees you: operator to chairman. | Chairing a monthly meeting rather than answering the phone at 9pm |
| 8 | **Value** | "More revenue means a more valuable business." | Growth that changes your multiple vs growth that doesn't. A group sells at a different number. | What the business is worth when someone serious asks |
| 9 | **The consolidator** | "Someone else will roll up our industry." | In every industry someone becomes the buyer everyone calls. Be that one. | Retiring owners ringing you first |
| 10 | **The room** | "That world, with bankers, boards and private equity, isn't for people like me." | Growth changes who you deal with. You earn the seat by owning the asset. | Across the table from private equity as the owner, not the employee |
| 11 | **Time** | "Massive growth takes a decade." | Ten years of organic growth vs 90 days to a signed LOI. Time is the real currency. | Being at the level in two years that peers reach in ten, if ever |
| 12 | **Who you become** | "Growth is about the numbers." | Massive growth is about who you have to become, and who it lets you be. | The group you hand on; the person your kids describe |
