# Revision brief v3: story-led, more analogies, deep brain-centre targeting

The 12 growth scripts in `growth-series/scripts/` are the base. This pass makes them **story-driven**, **richer in analogy**, and **deliberately engineered to hit every major brain centre**.

You are revising, not starting over. Keep each script's:

- title
- bottleneck
- the decomposition into its pieces
- the facts and proof
- the status scenes
- the soft link mention
- the exact closing invitation, as the last line

Every hard rule in `growth-series/script-brief.md` still applies. Read it again, plus `psywar-brain-guide.md` and `alen-growth-status.md`.

## 1. Make it story-based

**A spine story runs through the whole script.** Pick one of:

- an **illustrative owner, clearly made up**. Introduce him or her plainly, for example: "Let me paint you a picture. This owner's made up, but you'll recognise every part of him." Give them a first name, a trade, a town, a ute, a family and a Tuesday. Never present them as real, a client or someone Josh knows.
- **Josh's own sourced story**: 25, unemployed, about 1,000 cold calls, four failed deals, the physio clinic on seven years at four per cent with a nine-month holiday, the board found on LinkedIn.
- **A sourced friend or student, told as a story**, with the relationship exact.

Tell the spine story **in three or four parts spread across the script**:

1. Open it in the cold open.
2. Stop it at a moment of tension, an open loop.
3. Return to it after each piece of the decompile.
4. Resolve it near the end.

Never resolve the loop early.

**Add two or three shorter stories inside the script.** Use one personal confession from Josh (sourced) and one or two vignettes, illustrative or sourced.

**Write stories in scenes, not summaries:**

- place, time, one physical detail, one line of dialogue, and what the person felt in their body;
- present tense for the big moments;
- concrete nouns, such as the yard, the van keys, the whiteboard, the accountant's email, the phone face-down on the bench.

## 2. More analogies

- **Seven to nine analogies per script.**
- **The hero analogy becomes an extended metaphor thread.** Introduce it early, build it in the middle, flip it at the peak, and call it back at the close. That gives four touches.
- **The others are short, one to three sentences each,** placed exactly where a belief turns.
- **No two analogies in a row,** and each one is landed with a plain line that ties it back to the business.
- **No repeats.** Keep every analogy unique across this series and the belief-content scripts. Before adding one, grep both `growth-series/scripts/` and `belief-content/scripts/`. All the bans in `script-brief.md` still apply.

## 3. Go deep on the brain centres

Every script must deliberately hit **every** centre below at least once. The big ones (threat, reward and anticipation, social status, self) should be hit several times. **Never label them in the script.** Do the targeting through technique. Keep a private map while you write.

| Centre / system | What it responds to | How to hit it in Josh's voice |
|---|---|---|
| **Amygdala (threat, loss)** | Specific, personal, imminent loss; being left behind; a threat to the family | Name a concrete loss in the viewer's own world, e.g. "the contract you turned down in March, the one your competitor took". Use loss over gain. Show the competitor moving. Put fear early, then calm it. |
| **Insula (gut feeling)** | Felt body sensations | Describe what the stuck owner feels in the body: the tight chest at 2am, the stomach drop at the bank email, shoulders that never come down on holiday. Then describe the relief in the body after. |
| **Dopamine and nucleus accumbens (anticipation, reward prediction)** | Open loops, partial reveals, "in a minute I'll show you" promises, reward that exceeds expectation | Use 3–4 open loops per script, closed in order. Tease the mechanism before you reveal it. The peak should be bigger than expected: the flip. |
| **Hippocampus (memory, nostalgia)** | Recalled past experiences | "Remember the first year: the first invoice you wrote, the first person you hired." Link the new belief to a past win they already own. |
| **Mirror neurons (embodied simulation)** | Actions described step by step in the first or second person | Walk them through doing it: "You pick up the phone, you say the line, you hear him pause." Make the viewer rehearse the action in their head. |
| **Oxytocin (trust, bonding)** | Vulnerability, confession, a face, a shared struggle | Josh admits the four failed deals, the thousand calls, the fear. One warm, human moment per script, said slowly. |
| **Anterior cingulate (conflict, dissonance)** | Pattern interrupts and contradictions to what they believed | One or two sharp interrupts that contradict the old belief, then a resolution that removes the discomfort. |
| **Orbitofrontal cortex (value comparison)** | Side-by-side contrast | Contrast pairs in numbers and in life: two owners, two Tuesdays, two exits. Make the better option obvious without saying it. |
| **Social brain, TPJ and medial prefrontal (status, how others see you)** | Peer recognition, the respect of the industry, belonging to a group | The status scenes. Show how peers, suppliers, staff, a banker and the kids see the owner one level up. Belonging to "the owners who buy". |
| **Serotonin (pride, rank)** | Earned standing, being looked up to, quiet confidence | Pride in what they've already built, and the next rung as earned, never bought. |
| **Default mode network (self, future self)** | Self-referential reflection, imagining the future self | One slow future-pace scene, plus questions they answer about themselves. Identity lines: "You're the kind of owner who…". |
| **Prefrontal cortex (logic)** | Numbers, mechanism, a test they can run | The worked numbers, the 1.5 times rule and the test for tonight. Logic comes after emotion, to justify it. |
| **Cortisol relief, then calm (resolution)** | Threat named, then removed; certainty | Bound every fear with walls. End slow, still and certain. |

**Sequence, roughly:**

1. Threat, gut feeling and an open loop.
2. Memory and pride.
3. Dissonance.
4. Anticipation.
5. The story.
6. Mirror rehearsal.
7. The status scene.
8. Comparison.
9. The peak flip.
10. Logic.
11. Future self.
12. Calm close.

Vary this across your three scripts so they don't feel templated.

## Rules

- **Length:** 3,300–3,900 spoken words. Cut weaker lines to make room for story; don't pad.
- **Facts:** no new facts about Josh or named people. Illustrative characters and numbers are always clearly illustrative.
- **Delivery:** "See how that works?" at most once per script.
- **What to avoid:** exclamation marks, hype, fake urgency, envy, luxury and income flexing.
- **Voice:** calm Australian.
- **Format:** exactly the same as before, `# N. Title` then plain paragraphs. Overwrite your scripts in place.
