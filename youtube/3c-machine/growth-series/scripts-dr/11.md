The second piece is whose time you would be buying.

A whole generation of owners in traditional industries is getting ready to retire. Many of them have spent thirty years building exactly what you are planning to spend the next ten years building. And what they want most often is not the highest price. They want out, and they want to be looked after. They do not want to wait sixteen weeks for a bank to make up its mind.

Think about a relay race. The baton does not change hands with both runners standing still. The next runner is already moving when the handover happens, and the team does not lose a step.

That is what a good succession is. The retiring owner hands over a business that is already running. The customers keep ringing. The team keeps turning up. The year does not start from zero.

Here is an illustrative example. Say there is a business making two hundred and fifty thousand a year in profit. The rule that sits over the whole deal is one and a half times. The fourth piece is the time that does not come back.

Think about the ice in an esky on a hot weekend. You can leave the lid shut, never open it, never touch a thing. By Sunday afternoon it is still water sloshing round the bottom. Doing nothing did not save the ice. It just meant you never got to use it.

That is what waiting is. It feels like not losing. Nothing breaks. Nothing goes wrong. But the ice is melting whether you open the lid or not.
